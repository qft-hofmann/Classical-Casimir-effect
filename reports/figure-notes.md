# Numerical report figures

1. **Smooth-sphere force convergence.** Idea: a small algebraic residual does not
   remove geometry/trace error. Question: does the independently measured error
   decrease when the polygonal sphere is refined? Plot the relative error of the
   boundary-traction force against the analytic smooth-sphere force as a function
   of h/a, the maximum Euclidean triangle-edge length divided by sphere radius.
   Values h/a=0.618033988750, 0.324919696233, 0.164647160064 correspond to the
   unchanged 80-, 320-, 1280-triangle meshes. Force errors come directly from
   `examples/pilot/validation.json`; h/a is checked against generated vertices.
   Logarithmic axes expose the change in scale; connecting segments do not claim
   a proved convergence rate. A horizontal 1% line marks the practical target for
   illustrations, not an error bound. Dirichlet is blue/circles, Neumann red/squares.

2. **Independent force identity.** Idea: translating the scatterer and integrating
   boundary traction approach the same force under mesh refinement. Question:
   does their relative discrepancy decrease on the two tested meshes? Plot the
   absolute difference divided by the magnitude of the position-derivative force
   for the right sphere, k=a=1, g=2, transverse illumination. This is a numerical
   comparison at two meshes, not an error bound or a proof of the identity.
   The horizontal coordinate is h/a=0.324919696233, 0.164647160064 for the
   unchanged 320- and 1280-triangle meshes per sphere.

Both figures are editable PGFPlots/TikZ sources in the separate numerical report.
The captions state what was measured and the
limits of the evidence. Numerical CSV files and tables are generated by
`scripts/report_data.py`. Review and rendering records go in `output/report-qa/`.

Independent review on 29 October 2026 checked both rendered figures, their CSV
values against the original pilot JSON, measured mesh edges, notation and captions.
No corrections were requested. The 1% target is visibly distinct from measured
errors; tables and legends are readable at report width. Author review remains
separate. This update changes mesh annotations, not pilot measurements.


3. **Separation-force illustration.** Question: how does the computed force vary
   with separation at one fixed physical and numerical parameter set? Plot all
   150 verified Dirichlet samples against g/a, with the zero-force axis visible.
   The mesh is fixed at h/a ≈ 0.164647, k=a=A=1, d=(0,1,0). A thin line joins
   adjacent samples to guide the eye; it is not a decay fit or a convergence band.
   Source: `examples/dirichlet-ka1/forces.csv`, exported from hash-verified original
   cases. Rendering: `scripts/plot_examples.py`; report uses the same PNG.

Public-release review, 4 November 2026: corrected the distance figure's overlapping
legend and sample-count annotation by combining them in one legend. Independent
review of all three figures and seven rendered pages then passed: measured values,
mesh sizes, force normalization, equations and stated accuracy scope agree with
the evidence. No additional numerical measurements were introduced.
